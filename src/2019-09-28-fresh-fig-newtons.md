Figs! So many figs. (See [previous post](http://peachykeengreen.blogspot.com/)). We love fig bars... and oh look, my favorite vegan chef, Isa, has a recipe for [whole-wheat fig bars](https://www.isachandra.com/2010/09/whole-wheat-fig-bars/) and another for Fig Not-ins in Vegan with a Vengeance. She uses dried figs in both, but I want to use my fresh ones, so I mashed up her recipes with this [Fresh Fig Newton](https://www.sugardishme.com/fresh-fig-newtons/) recipe to get the following.**

[![](/images/bars.jpg)](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEgHXN2TdZX07xSPDZNQK-wtpBvc02nx7LMFX-h3FUYqZS3tCq0CAdo79etMb_N1QqJB1FGDcWD_LoBoGjF1kkhublPvjwIQ5A1gCeD-nnFRogdHM8L9OQQhVMYBNaweN2tQDc7gqQsE6VpT/s1600/bars.jpg)Ingredients****

Filling:

16 fresh figs, washed, dried, and quartered

1/4 cup brown sugar or maple syrup

1 T lemon zest

1 tsp vanilla

Dough:

2 C whole wheat flour

2 tsp vanilla

1/2 tsp baking powder

1/2 tsp baking soda

1/2 tsp salt

2 T flax meal

1/4 cup non-dairy milk

1/2 cup vegan margarine

3/4 C granulated sugar

Instructions**

Put filling ingredients into a medium saucepan. Bring to low boil, stirring as needed. Reduce heat and simmer 40-50 minutes, occasionally smashing larger pieces with back of a fork and stirring. They will cook down and thicken with a jam-like consistency. Remove filling from heat and cool completely. (Filling can be stored in the refrigerator for 3 days.)

In small bowl, mix together flax seed and non-dairy milk. In medium bowl, cream together margarine, sugar, and vanilla, and then add flax mixture. Add dry ingredients and stir to form a dough. I found that I needed to transfer the dough to a food processor and mix well there (adding a little more non-diary milk as needed) to get a dough that stuck together well; otherwise it was too crumbly. Gather into ball in your hands and divide into 2 or 4 sections. Cover with plastic wrap and refrigerate for at least an hour.

After dough has chilled, preheat oven to 350. Use silicon liners or lightly grease a baking sheet. On lightly floured surface, roll out each half of the dough into a rectangle roughly 9x15  and cut into 3 equal strips - OR create 6 balls, roll into tubes, and then roll each tube into a long thin strip (that worked well for me; see photos). Place 3 strips baking pan a few inches apart. Spread fig filling over dough, allowing 1/2 inch space on edges. Prepare top crust as you did the bottom, and place on top. Seal edges by lightly pressing with your fingertips.

Bake about 20-30 minutes until lightly browned. Let cool on baking sheet for 2 minutes, then cut each cookie to separate and transfer to cooling rack to cool completely.
